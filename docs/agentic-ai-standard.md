# Agentic AI governance standard (summary)

When an AI agent can act rather than only suggest, adopting it is a delegation of authority. The standard asks four things of every agent that can take consequential actions.

1. **Execution authority is explicit.** The decisions an agent may carry out without prior human approval are written down. They are approved by the person who owns those decisions, not just whoever configured the tool, and the record is kept outside the vendor's console.
2. **Someone can stop it.** A named person has the authority and the means to stop the agent, and the time it takes to stop it is known.
3. **A human is accountable.** Every agent identity maps to a named, accountable person for its consequential actions.
4. **Expanding authority is a decision.** Widening what an agent may do follows a defined process, with its own approval and record, and is re-reviewed on a schedule because agent behaviour can drift even when the rules don't.

This summary states the standard. The scored assessment questions and anchors that measure it are proprietary and not published here.
