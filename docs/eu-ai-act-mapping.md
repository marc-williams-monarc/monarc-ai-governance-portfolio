# EU AI Act: deployer and oversight mapping

Orientation only. This is not legal advice, and which duties apply depends on the system and the organization. Most business uses of AI are not high-risk under the Act.

| Provision | What it asks | Where the assessment looks |
|---|---|---|
| Article 4 (AI literacy) | Providers and deployers take measures to ensure sufficient AI literacy among staff who deal with AI systems | Whether staff who use or oversee AI have been prepared for it |
| Article 14 (human oversight) | High-risk systems are designed so that people can oversee them, including being able to stop them | Whether named people can understand, override and stop the systems they oversee |
| Article 26 (deployer obligations) | Deployers of high-risk systems use them according to instructions, assign competent human oversight, monitor operation, keep logs and inform affected workers | Named oversight, monitoring and record keeping in practice |
| Article 86 (explanation of individual decisions) | Affected persons can obtain an explanation of decisions taken on the basis of certain high-risk systems | Whether a specific decision can be explained: who decided, on whose authority, and why |

Scored controls are kept separate from procedural obligations, such as registration or notices, which need their own operational inventory.

Timing as published by the EU: AI literacy has applied since February 2, 2025. Transparency duties apply from August 2, 2026. Under the Digital Omnibus, high-risk obligations for Annex III systems apply from December 2, 2027.
